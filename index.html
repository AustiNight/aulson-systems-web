Principles

The classics never go out of style.

01

Single Responsibility

An AI agent that tries to do everything knows nothing deeply. Scope its context, define its purpose, and it becomes reliable. Expand it, and you get expensive confusion.

02

Open/Closed

Good AI architecture accepts new capabilities without rewiring what already works. Build in layers. The foundation should never know what gets stacked on top.

03

Liskov Substitution

If your system breaks when you swap the model, you built a dependency, not a system. The model is a component, replaceable. Your architecture is the product.

04

Interface Segregation

Bloated context is not thorough, it is expensive noise. A model given only what it needs outperforms one drowning in irrelevance. Precision beats volume.

05

Dependency Inversion

Most AI systems put the model in charge. The model should be a worker, not a manager. Build the logic in the architecture. Let the model execute it.
